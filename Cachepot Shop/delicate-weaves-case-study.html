02 — Problem Space

Challenge

Analysing Delicate Weaves revealed a significant gap between product quality and online presentation. Handcrafted rattan pieces of real artisanal value were hidden behind a generic, undifferentiated interface.

  • — Light design failed to communicate premium positioning
  • — No visual hierarchy guiding user attention
  • — Decorative elements distracted from products
  • — No clear brand differentiation from competitors
  • — Catalogue lacked product context & storytelling

Solution

A complete visual rebrand rooted in editorial aesthetics — using depth, restraint, and material references to create a premium digital experience that feels as refined as the products themselves.

  • ✦ Deep forest green creating immersive, premium atmosphere
  • ✦ Three-font typographic system establishing clear hierarchy
  • ✦ Asymmetric editorial grid with lifestyle photography
  • ✦ Guided catalogue journey replacing static product grids
  • ✦ Fully responsive, mobile-first implementation

09 — Layout

Breaking the Grid

Instead of the original site's static, symmetrical structure, the redesign introduces an asymmetric editorial grid — borrowed from luxury print design — that creates visual rhythm and surprise.

Each full-bleed photograph acts as a window into a refined interior, showing rattan in its natural habitat rather than isolated on white backgrounds.

01
Asymmetry creates movement and keeps the eye engaged across the page
02
White framing on photographs references gallery display — elevating product perception
03
Generous white space signals confidence and allows the products to breathe

What I Learned

◎
Brand ≠ product photos. The environment around the product — colour, typography, space — communicates more than the image itself. Premium feeling is built in the details.
◎
Two directions can coexist. Creating two distinct iterations — editorial asymmetric and structured grid — demonstrated that there is no single "correct" premium aesthetic. Context and audience matter most.
◎
Mobile is not a small desktop. Designing for touch, thumb reach, and vertical scrolling required fundamentally rethinking the layout — not just resizing it.
