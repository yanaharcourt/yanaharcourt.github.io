A Message From Our Director

Why This Report Matters

Dear Donors, Friends, Supporters, and Allies

We are honored to share with you our latest report, "Lived Realities of LGBTQ+ People in Tanzania." This report captures the voices of 367 LGBTQ+ individuals across the country and sheds light on their day-to-day experiences in education, healthcare, employment, housing, safety, and mental health.

Your support has made this possible. By standing with us, you help amplify these voices and push for change in systems that continue to marginalize our community. We hope this report serves as a critical resource in driving policy reform, legal protections, and inclusive programming.

Although Tanzania is a subscriber of the UN Human Rights Declaration, the government provides no protection against discrimination based on sexual orientation and gender identity. These findings make the urgency of that protection undeniable.

James Wandera Ouma Executive Director, LGBT Voice Tanzania

Healthcare Access

Healthcare discrimination is one of the most dangerous barriers facing LGBTQ+ Tanzanians — with life-threatening consequences.

78%

Denied or received substandard care

Respondents reported being turned away, given lesser treatment, or subjected to humiliating treatment by healthcare providers aware of their identity.

62%

Avoided seeking care due to fear

Fear of discrimination or outing themselves to medical staff caused many to delay or entirely forgo healthcare, worsening health outcomes.

44%

Denied HIV/sexual health services

Nearly half reported being refused STI testing, HIV medication, or sexual and reproductive health services specifically because of their orientation.

Education & Schools

Educational institutions should be places of safety and growth — yet for many LGBTQ+ youth in Tanzania, they become sites of harm.

52%

Expelled, suspended, or harassed at school

Over half of respondents experienced direct educational disruption related to their identity, often permanently altering their career prospects.

69%

Witnessed or experienced bullying

Physical and verbal bullying from peers — often tolerated or ignored by school staff — is a near-universal experience among LGBTQ+ students.

31%

Did not complete secondary education

Discrimination and unsafe environments led a significant portion to drop out, closing doors to economic independence and participation.

Mental Health

The psychological toll of sustained discrimination, rejection, and violence cannot be overstated — and access to affirming mental health care is nearly nonexistent.

71%

Experiencing depression or anxiety

The majority of respondents reported symptoms consistent with depression or anxiety directly attributed to discrimination and social exclusion.

38%

Reported suicidal ideation

Isolation, family rejection, and lack of support systems contribute to severe mental health crises across the LGBTQ+ community in Tanzania.

12%

Had access to affirming support services

Only a small fraction could access LGBTQ+-friendly mental health professionals, crisis support, or community programs — a critical gap that LGBT Voice Tanzania is working to address.
