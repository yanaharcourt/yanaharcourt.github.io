Who We Are

LGBT Voice Tanzania is a registered national LGBT organisation working to advance equality, diversity, education, and justice for LGBT people in Tanzania.

Support Our Work What We Do
Our History

How We Started

A desire for a more just society

In 2009, a group of LGBT+ individuals in Dar es Salaam founded LGBT Voice Tanzania out of a desire for a more just society that values diversity — where discrimination does not exist, where people live fully with pride and confidence, and celebrate their unique strengths.

Gays, Lesbians, Transgendered and Bisexual Tanzanians simply seek, and deserve, the same things all Tanzanians do:

  • To take care of each other and our families
  • To have good jobs
  • To support our neighbourhoods
  • To publicly serve our local, national, and religious communities

Since our founding, we have worked tirelessly in direct aid to individuals in crisis, healthcare access, political advocacy, and education & media.

Founded 2009

A group of LGBT+ individuals came together in Dar es Salaam with one shared goal — to build a country where every person can live freely and with dignity, regardless of who they love.

Our Foundation

Vision, Mission & Values

Everything we do is guided by a clear sense of purpose and a commitment to five core values.

Our Vision

A world where LGBT+ people can thrive free from the limitations of gender identity and sexual orientation oppressions.

Our Mission

Build a strong, vibrant future for the LGBT+ community — meeting the needs, advancing the rights, and celebrating the lives of Gay, Lesbian, Bisexual and Transgender people in Tanzania.

Our Values

All of LGBT Voice's work is guided by a commitment to five core values — from social justice and diversity to openness, inclusion, and ending discrimination.

01

Social Justice

We believe all people, regardless of sexual orientation or gender identity, have equal rights to a fair and equitable society.

02

Building Communities

Our strength is in community. We work to build relationships and organisations that serve LGBT people.

03

Diversity

We promote the idea that love, respect and acceptance should be afforded to individuals, regardless of who they are.

04

Openness & Inclusion

We form inclusive partnerships with government, policymakers, educational and religious institutions, and healthcare providers.

05

Ending Discrimination

Working toward a society where all sexual orientations and gender identities are treated with dignity, respect, and equality.

As a member of the LGBTQ community in Tanzania, I have faced numerous instances where I have been refused access to crucial public services solely because of my sexual orientation. This widespread problem has fueled my determination to find a solution.
James Wandera Ouma Executive Director, LGBT Voice Tanzania
The Reality

Homophobia in Tanzania

Although Tanzania is a subscriber of the UN Human Rights Declaration, the government provides no protection against discrimination based on sexual orientation and gender identity.

Homophobia takes many forms

All these forms of homophobia are destructive — not only for those who live openly as LGBT+, but for our country and society as a whole. Children are taught to hate from an early age, and the cycle of prejudice, violence, and hate continues, generation after generation. This must be stopped.

  • Verbal abuse and intimidation
  • Physical abuse
  • Expulsion from school
  • Discrimination at work
  • Psychological abuse
  • Anti-gay media production
  • Rejection from families
  • Sexual abuse
  • Denial of healthcare
  • Arbitrary arrest and imprisonment

We want the Tanzanian government to live up to its moral obligation under the UN Human Rights Declaration. We insist that all citizens are entitled to equal rights and protection under the law.

Our Demand

We hold the Tanzanian government accountable to international human rights obligations.

We want the Tanzanian government to live up to its moral obligation, according to the United Nations Human Rights Declaration. We insist that all citizens are entitled to equal rights and protection under the law.

We seek a country where all can enjoy "life, liberty, and security of person" — the very rights enshrined in international law that Tanzania has agreed to uphold.

Tanzania is a member and subscriber of the United Nations Human Rights Declaration, yet the government does not comply with its international human rights obligations and provides no protection against discrimination on the basis of sexual orientation and gender identity.
